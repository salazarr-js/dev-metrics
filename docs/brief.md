# dev-metrics — Brief

## North Star

**A personal mirror for my work as an engineer.**
One glance, every morning: what to push on today, and whether I'm getting better over time.

---

## The Problem

My work lives in two systems that don't talk to each other — Jira (what I'm supposed to deliver) and GitHub (what I'm actually doing). Neither answers the questions I wake up with:

- *What needs me today?*
- *Which of my PRs is stuck, and why?*
- *Am I going to hit this sprint?*
- *Am I improving — or am I just busy?*

So I drift. Overdue tickets slip. PRs rot waiting on a comment thread I forgot existed. Sprints end without me knowing if I committed too much or too little. Whole months pass with no sense of whether I got better.

---

## Why

I want to be **deliberate** about my craft, not reactive.

Reactive = checking Jira when someone asks, opening GitHub when an email pings, judging the week by how tired I feel.
Deliberate = starting the day with a clear shortlist, knowing my own rhythm, watching the trend line of how I work.

The tool exists to kill two specific failure modes:
1. **Dropping balls** — overdue tickets, abandoned PRs, ignored review requests.
2. **Invisible progress** — quietly getting better or worse with no way to know.

---

## What It Is

A small, local, single-user dashboard that fuses Jira and GitHub into three answers:

1. **What needs me today** — a short, ranked list of work that should actually move.
2. **How this sprint is going** — committed vs. done, with an honest read on whether the remaining days are realistic.
3. **How I'm trending** — month-over-month charts of how fast I ship, how clean my work is, and how I show up as a reviewer.

---

## Who It's For

Me. Only me. Not my manager, not my team. A private workspace I check the way other people check their inbox.

---

## Success

- I open it every morning before Jira or GitHub.
- The "needs me today" list is short and trustworthy enough that I act on it.
- I never again hear *"hey, what happened to that ticket?"* about something I forgot.
- At the end of each month, I can point to a chart and say *"this got better."*

---

## What It Is Not

- Not a team tool. Not a manager tool. Not a reporting tool.
- Not a replacement for Jira or GitHub — a lens on top of them.
- Not a productivity-shaming device. Trends exist to coach, not to punish.

---

## Principles

1. **One screen, one purpose.** If it doesn't help me decide what to do next or see how I'm trending, it doesn't belong.
2. **Today before history.** The top of the screen answers *"what now?"* History lives below.
3. **Honest signals.** No vanity metrics. If a number doesn't change my behavior, it isn't on the page.
4. **Private by default.** A mirror, not a window. Nothing leaves my machine.

---

## Nice to Have

**Peer comparison as a reference point.** Eventually, it'd be useful to see how my pace stacks up against other developers — commits, PRs opened, tickets closed, review turnaround. Not as a leaderboard or a competition, but as a quiet baseline: *am I keeping pace? where is there room to push?* A reference line on my own chart, not a ranking.
